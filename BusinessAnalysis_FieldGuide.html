The Business Analyst Field Guide

You are training to become an AI Solution Architect in the Colaberry Enterprise AI Leadership Accelerator. You will rarely write a full business requirements document yourself — an AI agent, an analyst, or a vendor will hand you one. Your job is to direct what gets built, evaluate whether a requirements artifact is good enough to build from, and approve it before it consumes engineering time. Foundations

The Discipline, in the 20% You Actually Need

Business analysis exists because the most expensive bug in any AI initiative is a requirement nobody wrote down correctly. A misunderstood requirement caught in a document costs an edit. The same misunderstanding caught in a demo costs a sprint. Caught in production, it costs an incident review and a regulator's phone call. As an AI Solution Architect, you are the checkpoint between an AI-generated (or human-drafted) requirements artifact and the engineering time it is about to consume — so you need enough of the discipline to read one critically, not to author one from scratch every time.

1Why BA Exists

Every system failure traces back to one of three gaps: the business need was never made explicit, the need was translated incorrectly into a technical requirement, or the requirement was correct but nobody verified the build actually satisfied it. Business analysis is the set of practices that closes those three gaps before code is written: elicit the real need, express it unambiguously, and make it testable. With AI-generated requirements now common, your job shifts from writing every artifact to directing what gets asked for, and catching what a fast AI draft gets wrong — vague acceptance criteria, missing non-functional targets, requirements with no owner, or a MoSCoW list where everything is conveniently a "Must."

2Functional vs. Non-Functional Requirements

Functional

What the system must do.

Example (ClaimsIQ): "The system shall auto-classify each First Notice of Loss into Low, Medium, High, or Catastrophic severity within 5 minutes of submission."

Non-Functional

How well the system must do it — performance, security, availability, accessibility, compliance.

Example (ClaimsIQ): "Severity classification shall complete at P95 ≤ 90 seconds under 300 concurrent submissions/hour, with automatic failover to the manual queue if the AI service is unavailable for more than 5 minutes."

Architect's flagAn AI-generated requirements draft that lists only functional requirements is incomplete by default. Every functional requirement that touches an external boundary, a regulated process, or a production system needs a paired non-functional counterpart: latency, availability, security, and auditability at minimum.

3User Stories

The standard shape is deliberately narrow: As a <persona>, I want <capability>, so that <business value>. The "so that" clause is not decoration — if you can't state the business value, the story likely shouldn't be built yet. A story is a placeholder for a conversation, not a finished spec; the acceptance criteria (next) are what make it buildable and testable.

4Acceptance Criteria — Given / When / Then

Acceptance criteria turn a story into something a tester (human or automated) can pass or fail with no ambiguity. The Gherkin shape — Given a starting state, When an action occurs, Then an observable outcome follows — forces specificity. "The system should be fast" is not acceptance criteria. "Given 300 concurrent submissions, When a claim is classified, Then P95 latency is ≤90 seconds" is.

5MoSCoW Prioritization

TierMeaningArchitect's test
MUSTLegally, contractually, or operationally non-negotiable for this release.Would we delay launch without it? If not, it isn't a Must.
SHOULDImportant, materially better outcome, but launch can proceed without it.Is there a workaround for release one?
COULDDesirable if time and budget allow; first to be cut under pressure.Does cutting it change the business case? If yes, it's mis-tiered.
WON'T (this release)Explicitly deferred, with a reason on record.Is the reason written down, or just implied?
Architect's flagIf more than roughly two-thirds of a requirements list is tagged Must, treat that as a defect in the draft, not a reflection of reality. Push back and force real trade-offs — see the User Stories and RTM documents for a realistic distribution.

6Personas

A persona is not a demographic profile. A useful persona names one role's goals (what they're trying to accomplish), frustrations (what currently gets in the way), and how the system changes their day. Age, and job title alone tell you nothing actionable; "Dana re-contacts policyholders for information that should have been captured at intake, which delays her weekly claim count" tells you exactly what to fix. See the Personas document.

7Use Cases

A use case documents a goal-directed interaction between an actor and the system in full: actors, trigger, preconditions, a numbered main flow, alternate flows (valid variations, e.g. a high-severity claim needing human review), exception flows (things going wrong, e.g. the AI service is down), and postconditions. A use case that only documents the happy path is a known-incomplete artifact — see the Use Cases document for worked alternate and exception paths.

8Traceability

Every requirement should be traceable forward to the user story or use case that implements it, and further to the test case that verifies it. This chain — need → requirement → story/use case → test case → verified status — is what a Requirements Traceability Matrix (RTM) captures. An orphan requirement (no story, no test) or an orphan story (no requirement behind it) is scope creep or a gap, and the RTM is the fastest way to find either.

9Good vs. Bad Requirements

 Bad (as commonly AI-drafted)Good (specific, testable, owned)
✗"The system should be fast and reliable.""P95 claim-classification latency ≤90s under 300 concurrent submissions/hour; automatic failover within 60s of a 5-minute AI-service outage."
✗"The system should detect fraud.""Claims with a fraud-indicator score above the configured threshold generate a referral packet listing each contributing indicator, delivered to the SIU queue within 15 minutes of claim creation."

10KPIs: Leading vs. Lagging

A lagging KPI confirms an outcome after the fact (e.g., annual fraud leakage). A leading KPI predicts it early enough to intervene (e.g., weekly fraud-referral precision). A good requirements set pairs at least one leading indicator with every lagging business outcome it claims to drive. See the KPI tiles in the Example Initiative and the Executive Summary.

11The Architect's Review Lens

When an AI agent hands you a requirements draft, run it through this checklist before you approve engineering time against it:

Ambiguity check

Could two competent engineers implement this differently and both claim compliance? If yes, it's not done.

Testability check

Is there a Given/When/Then or a measurable threshold? "Should be intuitive" is not testable; a task-completion rate is.

Traceability check

Does every requirement map to a business need and a test case? Any orphans on either end of the chain?

Non-functional completeness

Latency, availability, security, accessibility, and data retention addressed — not just the happy-path function?

Human-in-the-loop governance

For any AI-driven decision, is there an explicit line between what the model recommends and what a human decides? Is that line enforced in the requirement, not just implied?

Data lineage & explainability

Can every AI-influenced outcome be traced back to model version, inputs, and confidence — enough to survive an audit?

Bias & fairness

Could the model's inputs correlate with a protected class in a way that produces disparate outcomes (e.g., routing or fraud-scoring by geography)?

MoSCoW sanity

Is the Must list actually load-bearing, or is it inflated because prioritizing is uncomfortable?

Cost/complexity sanity

Does the smallest reasonable build satisfy the Must list, or has scope quietly expanded past the business case?

This checklist is deliberately the same shape as this guide's own BRD and RTM — use them as a worked example of what passing this review looks like.

The Worked Example

Meridian Mutual Insurance — the ClaimsIQ Initiative

Meridian Mutual Insurance is a regional property & casualty insurer with roughly 1.2 million policyholders across nine states, writing auto and homeowners lines. Every document in this guide is written for one initiative at Meridian: ClaimsIQ, an AI-assisted First Notice of Loss (FNOL) triage and routing system.

The problem, in one paragraph When a policyholder reports a loss today, a human triage team manually reviews it the next business day before assigning an adjuster — average time to assignment is 4.5 business hours, and up to 26 hours over a weekend. Manual severity scoring is inconsistent, missing an estimated 12% of claims that should be escalated same-day. Fraud indicators are flagged only when an individual adjuster happens to notice them, contributing to an estimated $4.2M/year in preventable leakage. Six of Meridian's nine operating states require claim acknowledgment within 24 hours by regulation; Meridian meets that bar only 91% of the time today.
The solution, in one paragraph ClaimsIQ ingests FNOL submissions from web, mobile, call center, and agent channels in real time; classifies severity; runs a hybrid rules-plus-ML fraud screen; and auto-routes each claim to the right adjuster by line of business, geography, and current workload — all within roughly 15 minutes. High-severity, catastrophic, and fraud-flagged claims are always routed through a human review step first. AI recommends; a human decides on anything consequential — full automated claim adjudication is explicitly out of scope for this release (see US-16, tagged Won't).

UC-01Submit FNOL and Receive Auto-Routed Triage

ActorsPolicyholder (primary), ClaimsIQ System, Claims Adjuster, Triage Exception Reviewer
TriggerPolicyholder reports a loss via web, mobile, call center, or agent channel.
PreconditionsPolicyholder has an active, in-force policy.

Frequency~330 times/day across all channels (peaks 3–5x during catastrophe events).

Main Flow

  1. Policyholder submits an FNOL via web, mobile app, call center, or agent portal.
  2. System validates that the policy is active and the loss date falls within the policy period.
  3. ClaimsIQ AI extracts structured claim data and classifies severity (Low/Medium/High/Catastrophic).
  4. ClaimsIQ AI runs the fraud-indicator screen in parallel and attaches a fraud score.
  5. Routing Engine auto-assigns the claim to an eligible adjuster by line of business, state license, and current lowest open-claim count.
  6. Adjuster is notified with the AI-drafted claim summary attached.
  7. Policyholder receives an acknowledgment (email/SMS) with claim number and expected next-contact window.

Alternate Flows

  • A1 — Missing required field. At step 1, if a required field is left blank, the system blocks submission (web/mobile) or blocks call progression (call center script) until the field is captured or explicitly marked "unavailable" with a reason code. Resumes at step 1.
  • A2 — High or Catastrophic severity. At step 3, if severity classifies as High or Catastrophic, the claim routes to the Triage Exception queue; an on-duty reviewer confirms or overrides the classification within 30 minutes, and a priority alert notifies a senior adjuster. Resumes at step 5 using the confirmed severity.
  • A3 — Fraud indicator threshold exceeded. At step 4, if the fraud score exceeds the configured threshold, a referral packet is generated and routed to the SIU queue in parallel with normal adjuster assignment (see UC-02). Adjuster assignment still proceeds at step 5; the SIU flag is visible on the claim record.

Exception Flows

  • E1 — AI triage service unavailable. If the AI service does not respond within 5 minutes, the claim automatically falls back to the legacy manual triage queue. The compliance acknowledgment clock still starts at submission time; an operations incident alert fires within 60 seconds.
  • E2 — Policy not found or lapsed. If policy validation fails at step 2, the claim routes to a Policy Verification queue for manual confirmation of coverage; the policyholder is notified of the delay and given a case reference number.

UC-02SIU Fraud Referral Review

ActorsSIU Investigator (primary), ClaimsIQ System, Claims Adjuster
TriggerA claim's fraud-indicator score exceeds the configured referral threshold.
PreconditionsClaim has completed AI fraud screening (UC-01, step 4).
PostconditionsClaim is placed on investigative hold, or proceeds to normal adjuster workflow with a dismissal reason logged.
Frequency~4% of claims generate a referral (~13/day).

Main Flow

  1. System flags a claim above the fraud-score threshold and generates a referral packet listing every contributing indicator.
  2. SIU investigator receives the referral with an evidence checklist.
  3. Investigator reviews the packet and either accepts or dismisses the referral.
  4. If accepted, the claim is placed on investigative hold and the assigned adjuster is notified.
  5. If dismissed, a reason code is required; the claim proceeds to normal adjuster workflow with no further SIU hold.

Alternate Flows

  • A1 — Additional information needed. At step 3, the investigator requests specific additional information from the assigned adjuster before deciding; flow pauses and resumes at step 3 once the adjuster responds.

Exception Flows

  • E1 — Incomplete evidence source. If a referral packet is generated with a missing or corrupt evidence source (e.g., a photo upload failed), the system logs a data-quality exception and notifies the data steward; the referral is still delivered to SIU with the gap explicitly noted, not silently omitted.

UC-03Compliance Monitoring of Acknowledgment SLA

ActorsCompliance Officer (primary), ClaimsIQ System, Claims Ops Manager
TriggerFNOL submission timestamp is recorded (UC-01, step 1).
PreconditionsClaim's applicable state acknowledgment window is known (18–72 hrs depending on jurisdiction).
PostconditionsAcknowledgment is sent within the required window, or a compliance exception is logged.
FrequencyContinuous, for every open claim.

Main Flow

  1. System timestamps FNOL receipt and determines the applicable state SLA window.
  2. System tracks whether and when an acknowledgment was sent to the policyholder.
  3. Compliance dashboard displays a live countdown per open claim against its SLA window.
  4. At 80% of the SLA window elapsed with no acknowledgment sent, the system alerts the Compliance Officer.

Alternate Flows

  • A1 — Claim nears breach. If a claim remains unacknowledged past the 80% alert with no action taken, the system auto-escalates to the Claims Ops Manager for manual intervention.

Exception Flows

  • E1 — Acknowledgment delivery failure. If the primary channel (email/SMS) bounces, the system automatically retries via a secondary channel (postal mail queue) and logs the failure for manual follow-up; the compliance clock is not paused by a delivery failure.
